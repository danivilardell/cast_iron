# Audit circuit (circuit 2) — implementation, numbers, and the curve decision

*2026-08-25. Packages: `shared/` (helpers both circuits use), `auditcircuit/` (this circuit, with a mocked
inner verifier), `auditcircuit/bw6verifier/` (soft BLS12-377-in-BW6-761 verifier prototype),
`voteropening/` (+ BLS12-377 configuration). Laptop numbers: Apple M1 Pro (10 cores, 32 GB), native arm64 Go
1.24.9, gnark v0.14.0, Groth16; cloud numbers: AWS x2idn.24xlarge (`plots/cloud_machine.txt`). The raw
measurement files (`results/`, `bw6verifier/results/`) are regenerated by the tests listed in § 7 and are not
shipped in the repository.*

## 1. What was built

**`shared/`** — factored out of the tallying circuit so the two circuits cannot drift: the Poseidon2 instance
(width 2, 6/50), `ExpByBits` / `ElGamalDecryptScalar`, `HashB3`, `RegTupleHash` (= voteropening's `TupleHash`
with `Dom = 0xC7C`), `CRegAlpha`, `CRegStep`, `EntryCommitment` (= voteropening's `EntryCommitment`, tag 1),
`MerkleRootFromPath` / `BuildPlainMerkle` (record tree), plus the native counterparts (`ComputeCReg`, ElGamal
keygen/encrypt/decrypt, …). The tallying circuit now calls these; its constraint count is unchanged (n = 8:
450,839 before and after) and its tests pass unchanged.

**`auditcircuit/`** — per ballot i, with the tallier's ElGamal key `sk` as witness:
1. decrypt `pkAx, pkAy, posReg, B3.Rx, B3.Ry, B3.S` from *the same ciphertexts* the tallying circuit decrypts,
   and the `ProofFields` ciphertexts of π_open (`Ballot.ProofC`, layout § 6);
2. `tupleHash_i = Poseidon(0xC7C, pkAx, pkAy, posReg, Poseidon(B3))` via `shared.RegTupleHash`;
3. record entry: `entry = Poseidon(1, posReg, rσ_hi, rσ_lo)` from the tallier's private witness
   (`RecordOpening`: the r-component of the transaction at `posReg` and its Merkle sibling path), folded up to
   the public `RecordRoot` (= the tallying circuit's `RegistrationRoot`, Mrk.Root(T_R)) — both the root
   comparison and the index range (`posReg < 2^depth`, checked on a canonical 254-bit decomposition) are soft;
4. `vOk = InnerVerifier.VerifyBit(π_open, tupleHash_i, entry)` (soft by contract);
5. `b_i = okKey · inRange · rootOk · vOk`, with `okKey = [H = G^sk]` (same soft key relation as the tallier), and
   `CReg = ∏_i (Poseidon(tupleHash_i, b_i) + alphaReg)`, `alphaReg = Poseidon(0xC7CA, eid, VoterRollRoot, RecordRoot)`,
   asserted equal to the public `CReg`.

Public inputs: `ElectionID, VoterRollRoot, RecordRoot, ElGamalG, ElGamalH, CReg` (the tallying circuit's public
context) and the bulletin board (6 + `ProofFields` ciphertexts per ballot). `InnerVerifier` is an interface;
`MockVerifier` (a Poseidon binding of the proof fields to the statement, so the bit really depends on the
decrypted proof and on the recomputed statement) stands in for the pairing-based verifier, `ConstVerifier` is
used for cost accounting only.

Tests (`go test ./auditcircuit/ .`): `TestHonest` (CReg equals `shared.ComputeCReg` on all-valid bits),
`TestInvalidOrAbsentProof` (random / all-zero π_open ⇒ b_i = 0 and the circuit *stays satisfiable*; the CReg with
b_i = 1 is rejected), `TestBadRecord` (wrong rσ, wrong path, out-of-range position ⇒ 0, satisfiable),
`TestTamperedTuple` (a changed registration field changes tupleHash_i: the old CReg is rejected, the proof bound
to the old tuple no longer verifies), `TestWrongKey`, `TestGroth16EndToEnd`, and in package main
**`TestCrossCircuitCReg`**: one synthetic scenario (4 voters, one bad registration, one bad anchor signature)
run through the tallying circuit and through the audit circuit on the very same ciphertexts, key and public
context — equal tuple hashes, equal bits, equal CReg, and the audit circuit rejects the CReg of a flipped bit.

**`auditcircuit/bw6verifier/`** — the native-recursion verifier prototype, see §§ 3–4.

**`voteropening/`** — untouched except for an additive `Config.Curve` (BN254 default, BLS12-377 optional): the
BLS12-377 configuration uses the same Poseidon2 parameters (explicitly constructed natively, since gnark-crypto's
BLS12-377 defaults differ) and passes all correctness tests on both curves.

## 2. Audit circuit numbers (BN254, mock verifier, 12 proof ciphertexts, record depth 33 ≈ Tron)

compiled n = 8, 64, 128 match it exactly).

**Per-ballot breakdown (40,388 constraints):**

| component | constraints | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| ElGamal decryption of the 6 registration fields (6 × 1,271) | 7,626 | 19 % |
| ElGamal decryption of the 12 π_open fields (12 × 1,271) | 15,252 | 38 % |
| record-tree membership: 33 levels × 374 (2 Poseidon permutations + selects) + 507 (canonical position bits) | 12,849 | 32 % |
| hashing: H(B3) 3 + tuple 4 + entry 3 + accumulator 2 permutations (186 each) | 2,232 | 5.5 % |
| **mock verification (placeholder, excluded from estimates)** | 2,421 | 6 % |
| misc (eqBits, products) | ≈8 | — |

Without the mock: 37,967 per ballot. With 24 proof ciphertexts (BN254 proof coordinates as two limbs): +15,252.
Depth 10 instead of 33: −8,602. Each ElGamal decryption re-decomposes `sk` into bits (507 of its 1,271
constraints); hoisting that once per circuit would save ≈9,100 per ballot (23 %) here and ≈13,000 per ballot in the
tallying circuit — not done, as it changes the shared gadget both circuits use.

**Parallelizability.** Ballots are independent and the accumulator is a product, so the audit splits into k
proofs over disjoint ballot chunks with `CReg = ∏_k CReg_k` verified natively (k field multiplications) — no
recursion needed for horizontal scaling; the same trick applies to any subset partition. Within one proof gnark's
Groth16 prover already uses all cores (MSMs, FFTs), as the 96-vCPU run of the tallying circuit showed.

## 3. Cost of native in-circuit verification (`bw6verifier/`)

A soft verifier for Groth16 proofs of BLS12-377 circuits inside a BW6-761 circuit (native 2-chain), for a
proof shaped like the voter proof (2 public inputs, 1 Pedersen commitment):

| variant | BW6-761 R1CS constraints |
|---|---:|
| **soft verification with subgroup checks (sound against a malicious tallier)** | **59,158** |
| soft verification without subgroup checks | 40,363 |
| gnark's asserting `AssertProof` (no subgroup checks) | 34,520 |

Groth16 on BW6-761 for one such verification: setup 13.6 s, **prove 1.27 s**, verify 8 ms. Wiring it into the audit
circuit means the audit circuit lives on BW6-761 (§ 5): per ballot ≈ 59k (verification) + the decryptions,
hashing and record check re-expressed over the 377-bit field (≈1.5× their BN254 counts) ≈ 100–110k BW6-761
constraints ≈ 1–2 s per ballot on this machine, i.e. **10–20× the BN254 mock estimate**; n = 512 ≈ 10–20 min.

`TestSoftVerifier` checks: valid proof → 1; wrong statement → 0; off-curve point → 0; absent (all-zero) proof →
0 — all four satisfiable. The emulated alternative (a BN254 proof verified inside a BN254 circuit with gnark's
emulated pairing, `TestEmulatedBN254Cost`): **1,544,180 constraints** per verification (compile-only) — ruled out.

**Deployment requirement:** the inner (voter) proof must be produced with
`groth16.Prove(…, stdgroth16.GetNativeProverOptions(outerField, innerField))` — the Pedersen-commitment challenge
is a hash-to-field that the recursive verifier recomputes with a MiMC-based hash, not gnark's default SHA-256
one; a proof made with default options verifies natively but not in-circuit.

**Voter circuit on BLS12-377** (the inner curve of native recursion): 24,528 constraints, prove 0.176 s, verify 2.9 ms,
proof 292 B (raw 580 B), pk 9.0 MB — vs BN254: 22,920, 0.112 s, 1.9 ms, 196 B, 5.8 MB. The +7 % is Poseidon2's
degree-17 S-box on BLS12-377 (vs 5 on BN254).

## 4. How verification is made soft (soundness-critical; `bw6verifier/verifier.go`)

gnark's recursive verifier only asserts. `VerifyBit` reproduces its computation with every check turned into a bit:
1. *Well-formedness without divisions*: on-curve `y² = x³ + b` (rejects (0,0), i.e. an absent proof) for A, C, the
   commitment and its PoK; on-twist for B; then prime-subgroup membership through gnark's own characterisations
   (`P = −[x₀²]φ(P)` on G1, `ψ(Q) = [x₀]Q` on G2) but computed with *complete* unified additions, so that a
   small-order point supplied by a malicious voter cannot make the tallier's proof unsatisfiable.
2. *Gate*: every proof point is replaced by `Select(wellFormed, point, dummy)` where `dummy` is a constant valid
   proof, so the MSM and the Miller loops (incomplete formulas) only ever see prime-order points.
3. *Equations as bits*: the Pedersen check `e(C, −G/σ)·e(π, G) = 1` and the main equation
   `e(K, −γ)·e(C, −δ)·e(A, B) = e(α, β)` are evaluated with `Pair` and compared with a full 12-coordinate GT equality.
   **gnark v0.14.0's `sw_bls12377.Pairing.IsEqual` is buggy** (it compares 8 of the 12 coordinates — `C0.B0.A0`,
   `C0.B1.A1`, `C1.B0.A0`, `C1.B1.A1` twice each, never the `B2` components) and must not be used for this.
4. `bit = wellFormed ∧ commitmentOk ∧ mainOk`.

Completeness: a valid proof gives 1 (`TestSoftVerifier`). Soundness: with `wellFormed = 1` the checks are exactly
the standard verifier's on the same points; with `wellFormed = 0` the bit is 0 regardless. The mock verifier in
`auditcircuit/` has the same contract (bit from a soft equality; never unsatisfiable).

## 5. Status and limitations

*Inner verifier.* The verification of π_open inside the audit circuit is mocked (`MockVerifier`): every other
component — decryption under the tallier's key, the tuple hash, the record-tree membership check, the accumulator —
is the real one, and the measured cost of an in-circuit verification is reported separately (§ 3) rather than
included in the per-ballot numbers.

*Curve.* gnark's native recursion (BLS12-377 proofs verified in a BW6-761 circuit) is supported and is what
`bw6verifier/` measures, but a BW6-761 audit circuit computes over BLS12-377's *base* field, so it cannot natively
decrypt the BN254 ElGamal ciphertexts of the bulletin board nor recompute the BN254 Poseidon tuple hash and
accumulator that the tallying circuit uses; emulating either costs 10⁵–10⁶ constraints per ballot. Integrating the
real verifier therefore requires either moving the tallying and audit circuits to BW6-761 with voter proofs on
BLS12-377 (≈100–110k BW6-761 constraints per audited ballot, § 3), or dropping in-circuit verification in favour of
publicly posted, per-ballot re-randomised opening proofs with blinded statements, verified natively by anyone.
Verifying a BN254 proof inside a BN254 circuit with an emulated pairing costs 1,544,180 constraints per proof and is
not an option.

*Accumulator.* Both circuits bind the per-ballot bits through `Poseidon(tupleHash_i, b_i)`; adding the ballot
position to the hash (one line in `shared.CRegStep`, +186 constraints per ballot in each circuit) would make the
binding per ballot rather than per multiset of registrations.

*Record depth.* The record tree is assumed to have 2^33 leaves (the whole Tron chain); the membership check is 32 %
of the per-ballot cost and scales linearly with the depth.

## 6. Deviations from the paper's Appendix C / Definition 3

- The registration-validity check of Definition 3 (`CTC.Open` inside the tallying relation) is split across the
  voter proof, this audit circuit and the accumulator link; the tallying relation's public input list therefore
  gains `CReg` and loses the registration path, and Mrk.Root(T_R) is verified here, not in the tallier.
- The record entry is bound through a Merkle path supplied by the *tallier* as private witness against the public
  root (the voter's proof is root-independent, as it must be: the record grows between registration and tally).
- The key relation, index range and record membership are soft predicates feeding `b_i`, not assertions.
- The in-circuit verifier is mocked; its real cost is § 3.

